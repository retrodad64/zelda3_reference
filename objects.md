# Object reference

The things that are neither sprites nor tiles. Sprites are in `enemy_combat.md`
and `boss_combat.md`, tile behaviour is in `ground_types.md`, and rooms are in
`dungeon_rooms.md`.

Generated by `tools/object_reference.py`. Timers are frame counts, and a frame
is about a sixtieth of a second.

## Ancillae

a transient object with no health and no sprite slot: a bomb, an arrow in

## Bomb damage

The bomb is the one ancilla with its own damage tables. What it costs Link
depends on his mail, and how long it stuns him depends on how close he was:

<sub><code>src/ancilla.c</code></sub>
```c
    static const uint8 kBomb_Dmg_Delay[16] = {32, 32, 32, 32, 32, 32, 24, 24, 24, 24, 24, 24, 16, 16, 16, 16};
    static const uint8 kBomb_Dmg_ToLink[3] = {8, 4, 2};
```

So 8, 4 and 2 against green, blue and red
mail, which is one heart down to a quarter. The stun runs from
32 frames (0.53s) at the centre to
16 frames (0.27s) at the edge, in three bands.

## How a weapon hurts an enemy

Contact damage to Link is one table. Damage the other way is two, and the pair
is why a given weapon does nothing to some enemies:

<sub><code>src/sprite.c</code></sub>
```c
  uint8 dmg = kEnemyDamages[damage_type_determiner * 8 | enemy_damage_data[sprite_type[k] * 16 | damage_type_determiner]];
```

`damage_type_determiner` is the weapon. `enemy_damage_data` is a 16 entry row per
sprite type saying how that enemy reacts to each weapon, and `kEnemyDamages` turns
the reaction into a number. A row entry of zero means immune, which is how
Mothula ignores the best sword until the bug fix patches its row.

## Prize packs

What a cut bush, a lifted rock or a dead enemy leaves behind. Seven packs of
eight, rolled on at the moment the thing is destroyed:

| Pack | Always drops | Contents |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | no | `D8` `D8` `D8` `D8` `D9` `D8` `D8` `D9` |
| 1 | no | `DA` `D9` `DA` `DB` `DA` `D9` `DA` `DA` |
| 2 | no | `E0` `DF` `DF` `DA` `E0` `DF` `D8` `DF` |
| 3 | yes | `DC` `DC` `DC` `DD` `DC` `DC` `DE` `DC` |
| 4 | no | `E1` `D8` `E1` `E2` `E1` `D8` `E1` `E2` |
| 5 | no | `DF` `D9` `D8` `E1` `DF` `DC` `D9` `D8` |
| 6 | no | `D8` `E3` `E0` `DB` `DE` `D8` `DB` `E2` |

The mask decides whether the roll can come up empty:

<sub><code>src/sprite.c</code></sub>
```c
      if (!(GetRandomNumber() & kPrizeMasks[prize])) {
```

A mask of 1 keeps the low bit of a random number, so those packs drop about half
the time. Pack 3 has a mask of 0, so it always drops.

Only three of the prize types have a handler of their own:

- `D8` Heart
- `D9` Green Rupee
- `E3` Fairy

The rest share `Sprite_D9_GreenRupee` as their handler, with the type deciding
the graphic and the effect, so the source does not name them individually.

The bee is not in these packs. It is sprite `79`, with its own handler
`Sprite_79_Bee`, and a second form `B2` used once Link has caught one.

## What is not here

Inventory items and the abilities they grant. Those are not a table: each item
is a branch in the player code, and turning them into a list means reading those
branches rather than reading data.

Keys and the doors they open. A door's state lives in the room header and the
save file, not in an object, and the association is made when the room loads.
